RumptyCloud is a cloud provider based in Lagos, Nigeria. It runs apps, Linux VMs, managed databases, S3-compatible storage, Kubernetes and AI inference on about 90% renewable energy. I redesigned their marketing site, docs, blog and the dashboard.

## What I did

I was tasked with the redesign. It started with the marketing site, which was built with Astro, and for the redesign I picked Next.js. I also brought the docs and the blog over to the new Next.js site, so everything is under one domain. The old docs were Docusaurus, and the new docs are simply Next.js with Fumadocs. The old blog was Astro, and now it's on the site as well.

After that, I redesigned the dashboard, with a focus on its user experience.

## Problem

The site was previously an Astro site, vibecoded in a day with Claude. It didn't look very nice, and they wanted it to look a lot better and still capture what they're trying to sell.

That's all I was given. I didn't have much to go on. All I had was the current site and the idea of what they're trying to sell. It's an infra company, it's supposed to be eco-friendly, and that's basically it.

## Process

### Direction

The direction I took was to look at current infra companies and how I think they should look and feel. [Vercel](https://vercel.com), [Railway](https://railway.com) and [Fly.io](https://fly.io) are examples, among others. I looked at how they present what they offer on their sites. Then I got an idea of what direction to head in.

### Method

I would give an idea to Claude, and we would spin up various variations of something. We take it section by section, the hero section first, and we design multiple variations of that section in [Paper](https://paper.design). Then I take a look, and if I don't like something, I ask for more options or give it alternate directions. We also mix and match the options and keep iterating until we get a section that looks good.

With each section and its variation, I had a lot of things I rejected and some that survived. I then took all those decisions and put them in a `Design.md` making it less likely for the Agents to make the same mistakes and easier  to keep things consistent.


## Visual language

The colors on the old site were all over the place. There was no cohesion and no proper hierarchy. There were awkward fonts, awkward spacing, borders everywhere, and animations that didn't convey anything. So I stripped it all down and made it as simple as possible. Once we have the visual language down, we can become increasingly more elaborate with the designs and animations.

This is the methodology I took for the designs and the sketching, and how we iterated and pulled something together.

## Docs

The docs redesign was much easier, since the docs are heavily editorial and mostly text. The old docs were a Docusaurus site, and they only needed a similar look to the website, so there wasn't really much to do. They had some unnecessary tabs in the row below the header, which I got rid of, and that gives the new docs more vertical space.

The old docs were on a subdomain, but we brought the whole thing together so it's easier to keep in one place and to keep continuity in the design.

## Dashboard (in progress)

Currently I'm working on the dashboard, and it's going to be a lot harder. The dashboard has as many pages as the marketing site, and the problem is that every part is moving, unlike the marketing site, which is static. The dashboard has error states and loading states, and I have to redesign it around their current APIs without breaking anything.
